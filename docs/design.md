# Lindenfret: Design

## Summary

Lindenfret is a Python package that turns a seed and a config file into a playable variant of Villa-Lobos's Etude 1. Each variant keeps the original's right-hand pattern and rhythm. Everything else is generated from the seed, and nothing is copied from the original. An L-system generates the form, harmony, modulations, planing shapes and ending, and a fretboard voicer makes sure every bar can be played. Each run writes MIDI and MusicXML notation, plus a manifest that regenerates the piece exactly.

The musical model comes from an analysis of a MIDI of the original, summarized under [The reference](#the-reference-etude-1). The build plan is in [plan.md](plan.md).

## What it produces

- **One guitar part** in 4/4 and standard tuning, written in standard notation with no key signature.
- **A moving tonal center.** Each piece starts on a center chosen by the seed, and the grammar modulates within and between sections. Nothing pins a piece to E minor or to any one key.
- **An unbroken 16th-note arpeggio in Etude 1's right-hand pattern**, 16 notes per bar. Each bar is played twice, as in the original.
- **A form generated by the L-system**, modeled on the original's A B A′:
  - Graph-mode sections establish a center, and later move to a new one or return.
  - Between them, a planing sequence, which has no center, slides a generated four-finger shape along the neck against open strings.
- **A generated ending.** A piece ends where its last section's derivation ends. Rules can close a section with a cadence to its current center, but no chords are fixed.
- **Playable fingerings in every bar**: a span of at most 5 frets, nothing above fret 13, at most 4 fretting fingers.
- **Three files** in `out/etude1-<seed>/`:
  - `piece.mid`, with let-ring note lengths and one MIDI channel per string.
  - `piece.musicxml`, standard notation that opens in MuseScore.
  - `manifest.json`, the record used to regenerate the piece.
- **Reproducibility**: the same seed, config and code version always give byte-identical MIDI and manifest. The MusicXML has the same notes, but its encoding date and the part IDs music21 generates change on every run.

## The reference: Etude 1

Source: [reference/Villa-Lobos_Etude_No1b.mid](../reference/Villa-Lobos_Etude_No1b.mid), 62 bars on a single guitar track. `tools/analyze_reference.py` re-derives everything below.

| Aspect | In the reference |
| --- | --- |
| Meter and key | 4/4, centered on E minor throughout |
| Rhythm | 16 sixteenth-note onsets per bar in bars 1–59; bar 60 is in triplet eighths, and bars 61–62 are rolled chords |
| Right-hand pattern | Strings 6 4 5 3 4 2 3 1 2 1 3 2 4 3 5 4. Under this order, 54 of the 59 arpeggio bars put exactly one pitch on each string |
| Repetition | Each bar is played twice in bars 1–44 and 47–54 |
| Left hand | Stopped notes never go above fret 12. In the 54 bars that follow the pattern, the fretted span is at most 3 frets. 34 of the 59 arpeggio bars start on the open low E. The natural harmonics in bars 59–60 don't count toward the range |
| Tempo | ♩ = 126 for bars 1–20, ♩ = 120 from bar 24; ritardandos over bars 21–23, 44–46, and from bar 56 to the end |

Form, bar by bar:

| Bars | Harmony and texture | Left hand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–12 | Em, F♯ø7/E, Em, B7/F♯, Em/G, E/G♯. The bass climbs E, F♯, G, G♯ | Open position, open strings |
| 13–22 | Am, a B♭-bass chord (B♭ E G D A), Em/B, Bsus4, B7. The bass continues A, B♭, B | Barre shapes at frets 5–9 |
| 23–44 | Planing: a G♯°7 shape over open low and high E, sliding down one fret every bar pair | Strings 5, 4, 3, 2 at frets b+1, b+2, b, b+2, with b falling from 10 to 0; strings 6 and 1 open |
| 45 | The zig-zag keeps climbing: after the first 9 notes it goes on up the E-minor triad (G4 E4 B4 G4 E5) instead of turning back, then ends with a D♯–E turn | Up the neck on the top strings, reaching fret 12 on string 1 |
| 46 | A patterned descent in straight 16ths: each triad tone from B4 down to G2 is approached from a semitone below (A♯–B, F♯–G, D♯–E, repeated through three octaves). The approach notes are doubled, the chord tones accented | Down the neck across all six strings |
| 47–54 | F♯7, C♯ø7/G, F♯°7, B7: the approach back to E | Frets 2–9 |
| 55–58 | Coda on an E-minor pedal, alternating with C/E whose G moves to B♭ | Open position |
| 59–60 | The climbing zig-zag again, from E2 up to G5, continuing in bar 60 in triplet eighths (E5 B5 G5) to a held E6 | Ends in natural harmonics: bar 59's top notes and all of bar 60 match the harmonics at frets 12 and 5 on strings 1–3. Not counted in the range |
| 61–62 | Closing chords, both rolled: Am7, then E6 | — |

**The pattern is a rule.** The 16-note pattern is a zig-zag over the chord's six notes, low to high. It goes up two, back one to the top, then down two, up one. Bars 45, 59 and 60 apply the same zig-zag but keep climbing through higher chord tones instead of turning around. Bar 46 uses a different cell, a semitone approach and then a chord tone, sequenced down the triad. Every bar that departs from the pattern is still built from the chord's tones.

What the generator takes from this:

- **Pattern and rhythm** exactly as above, each bar played twice.
- **Minor-mode palette** from the chords in bars 1–22 and 47–54, written in E and transposed to whatever center is current. The graph weights favor stepwise bass motion, like the climb from E to B in bars 1–22.
- **The planing idiom** from bars 23–44: a four-finger shape sliding one fret per bar pair against open strings. The shape itself is generated; the reference's G♯°7 shape is one example of what the generator can choose.
- **Voicer preferences**: a span of 3 frets or less, and open strings, as the reference plays. Both are soft preferences; the hard limits stay at 5 frets and fret 13.
- **Tempo**: ♩ = 120 by default, with a ritardando over the last bar pair of each section.

What it doesn't take:

- **The key.** The reference stays centered on E; generated pieces move between centers.
- **Anything outside the core pattern section.** The generator models bars 1–44 and 47–58, where every bar plays the 16-note pattern. The climbing zig-zag and the semitone-approach descent (bars 45–46 and 59–60) and the rolled closing chords (bars 61–62) are left out for now.
- **Any fixed material.** No chords, shapes or passages are copied into a generated piece. The reference supplies vocabulary and tendencies, not content.

## Goals, constraints and non-goals

Goals:

- Every bar is playable on a standard-tuned guitar, verified by explicit rules rather than best effort.
- The L-system audibly shapes the piece: changing a rule changes its structure and its path through keys, not just its length.
- Seed plus config reproduce a piece exactly, and every output carries the config that made it.
- MIDI that rings like a guitar, and standard notation to play from.
- Experiments happen in config files and git branches, not by copying code.

Constraints, fixed for now:

| Constraint | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Target | Etude 1 only |
| Right-hand pattern | Etude 1's, fixed |
| Rhythm | The original's: 16 sixteenth notes per bar in 4/4, each bar played twice |
| Key | No key signature and no fixed tonal center; the center moves |
| Tempo | ♩ = 120 by default |
| Left-hand span | Highest fretted note minus lowest fretted note ≤ 5 frets; open strings don't count |
| Highest fret | 13 |
| Outputs | MIDI and MusicXML (standard notation), plus a manifest used only for regeneration |
| Python | Homebrew `python3` 3.14 in a venv; a conda env only if a different version is ever needed |

Non-goals, for now:

- Other etudes, or right-hand patterns other than Etude 1's.
- Rhythmic variation.
- Tab output, audio rendering or a GUI.
- Learning style from scores or recordings.
- Tunings other than standard.

## Architecture

A plain-Python core plans every bar and knows nothing about music21. At the end, `score.py` builds the notation with music21, and `export.py` writes it along with the MIDI (via mido) and the manifest.

```
                    configs/etude1.toml  +  seed
                                |
                                |  one RNG stream per stage
                                v
+------------------- core: plain Python, no music21 -------------------+
|                                                                      |
|  lsystem.py     expand the form grammar and each section grammar     |
|       |         -> symbol strings                                    |
|       v                                                              |
|  interpret.py   length control: first iteration inside the range     |
|       |         -> SectionPlan per section                           |
|       v                                                              |
|  harmony.py     walk each section: centers, chords, planing offsets  |
|       |         -> Bar with Center and ChordSpec                     |
|       v                                                              |
|  fretboard.py   graph bars: enumerate fingerings, reject, score      |
|  planing.py     planing bars: generate a shape, slide it             |
|       |         -> Fingering per bar                                 |
|       v                                                              |
|  render.py      Fingering x Etude 1 right-hand pattern, let-ring     |
|                 -> NoteEvents (onset, duration, string, fret, pitch) |
|                                                                      |
+-------------------+-------------------------------------+------------+
                    |                                     |
                    v                                     v
+----- music21 boundary --------------+   +----- report.py (inspect) ---+
|  score.py   one guitar part,        |   |  derivation, centers,       |
|             no key signature        |   |  chord plan, fingerings     |
|  export.py  .mid  .musicxml  .json  |   |  never imports music21      |
+-------------------------------------+   +-----------------------------+
```

Each stage reads its own section of the config and its own RNG stream ("form", "section.<n>", "harmony", "voicer", and "planing.<n>" for each planing section), and hands a plain dataclass to the next. `pipeline.py` runs the stages in order. `chords.py` parses the palette's chord symbols with music21 and transposes them once, before planning; after that the core works with plain values and never touches music21.

### Data model

| Type | Holds | Made by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Pattern` | the string and velocity for each of the 16 notes, and the note value | `config.py` |
| `SectionPlan` | the section, its position in the form, the derivation that fit its length range (axiom first), attempts | `interpret.py` |
| `Bar` | section, center, chord, fret region, role (tonic, step, hold, dominant, planing), events since the last bar, fingering | `harmony.py`; fingering by `pipeline.py` |
| `Center` | tonic pitch class and mode (minor or major); none during planing | `harmony.py` |
| `ChordSpec` | root, quality, spelled pitches, required tones, allowed tensions, bass tone | `chords.py` |
| `Shape` | a planing shape: fret offset per string from the base fret (open strings marked), and the kinds it qualifies as | `planing.py` |
| `Fingering` | fret and MIDI pitch per string (0 = open) | `fretboard.py` |
| `NoteEvent` | onset, duration, string, fret, MIDI pitch, velocity, spelled name | `render.py` |
| `Measure` | number, start, the bar it plays, its notes, directions such as rit. | `render.py` |
| `Piece` | seed, form derivation, section plans, bars; written out as `manifest.json` | `pipeline.py` |

### The L-system alphabet

Two grammar levels. A form grammar yields section letters. Each section letter has its own bar grammar, whose symbols are musical actions. The right-hand pattern and rhythm never change; the grammar moves the harmony, the tonal center and the left hand.

| Symbol | Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `A`, `B`, `C` … | form | a section; its profile (fret region, harmony mode, length) comes from config |
| `F` | bar | one bar, played twice. Graph mode: take one step along the chord graph; a section's first bar plays its tonic. Planing mode: play the shape where it is |
| `H` | bar | one bar, played twice. Graph mode: hold the chord and re-voice it. Planing mode: same as `F` |
| `M` | bar | modulate: move the tonal center one step along the center graph; the next bar picks a chord in the new center, weighted by bass motion |
| `+` / `-` | bar | move the fret region, or the planing shape, up or down one fret; a region never moves past fret 0 or `max_fret` |
| `[` / `]` | bar | save / restore harmonic state (chord, center and fret region): an excursion that returns to where it started |
| `K` | bar | cadence: two bars, the current center's dominant and then its tonic |

Brackets are the key feature. A bracketed L-system nests departures and returns, which maps directly onto excursions to other keys that come back, or don't, depending on the rule.

Rules are weighted alternatives, written in the config. The form and two sections of `configs/etude1.toml`; the rest of the file adds section C and sets the meter, pattern, fretboard limits, voicer, centers, palette and chord graph:

```toml
[form]
axiom = "S"
iterations = 1
rules.S = [
  { to = "A B A", weight = 3 },
  { to = "A B C A", weight = 1 },
]

[sections.A]
axiom = "F"
rules.F = [
  { to = "F[+F]F", weight = 2 },
  { to = "F[MF]F", weight = 1 },
  { to = "FMF", weight = 1 },
  { to = "FFK", weight = 1 },
]
length = [6, 12]         # bars before repetition
harmony = "graph"
region = "0-9"

[sections.B]
axiom = "F"
rules.F = [
  { to = "F-F", weight = 3 },
  { to = "F[-F]F", weight = 1 },
]
length = [6, 12]
harmony = "planing"
region = "0-12"              # the shape's start fret is drawn from here
shape_types = { dim7 = 3, augmented = 2, any = 1 }   # weights for the generated shape's chord type
open_strings = 2             # how many strings ring open; which ones is generated
```

**Length control.** A section grammar iterates until its count of bar symbols lands inside `length`, so every derivation is used whole. If an iteration overshoots, it re-derives from the next draw of that stage's RNG, with a retry limit. Past the limit it fails with a message naming the section. `inspect` prints the count per iteration so rules and ranges can be tuned together.

### Tonal center

A `Center` is a tonic pitch class plus a mode, minor or major. It is harmonic state, like the current chord, and nothing keeps it fixed.

- **Start**: drawn from `[centers] start` and `start_mode`. The default weights favor centers whose tonic is an open string (E, A, D, G, B), because open strings then ring as chord tones. Any of the 12 is possible.
- **Moves**: `M` moves the center one step along the center graph. The steps are stay, up or down a fifth, relative major or minor, parallel mode, up or down a major third keeping the mode (a chromatic mediant), and up or down a semitone. Each is weighted in `[centers] moves`. Only moves to a center with a chord playable in the current fret region are offered; if none is, `M` stays put and says so.
- **Excursions**: `[` and `]` save and restore the center along with the chord, so an excursion to another key returns only where the grammar brackets it.
- **Between sections**: each graph section starts one move from where the previous graph section ended, chosen among moves whose tonic is playable in the new section's region. "Stay" is one of the moves, so returning to the opening center is possible but never required. If no move fits, the section draws a new starting center.
- **Planing** has no center. The shape's pitches are whatever it and the open strings produce.
- **Ending**: a piece ends wherever its last section's derivation ends, on whatever center it has reached. Nothing forces a return to the starting center or a final cadence; a `K` in the rules produces one.

### Harmony: graph mode and planing mode

- **Graph mode** thinks in chords relative to the current center. The palette and its graph are written in E and transposed to the center when used; minor and major centers each have their own palette. The weights favor stepwise bass motion. Sections start on their center's tonic, `K` cadences to the current center, and `M` modulates.

  Tonic and dominant chords come from the palette, not the config. The tonic chords are those rooted on the tonic with the mode's third (Em, Em/G and Em/B in minor). The dominants are chords on the fifth with a major third (B7 and B7/F♯; Bsus4 has no third). The walk picks among whichever of them are playable in the current region. If none is, the bar takes an ordinary step instead and `inspect` notes why.
- **Planing mode** thinks in hand shapes. A `Shape` holds fret offsets per string relative to a base fret, with some strings marked open, and `+` and `-` move the base fret. The shape slides along the neck while open strings ring against it. This idiom is central to Villa-Lobos's guitar writing, and choosing chords by name can't produce it.

  Each planing section generates its own shape, in `planing.py`, from its own random stream:
  - **Candidates**: every way to leave `open_strings` strings open and fret the rest at offsets from a base fret, within the hard limits. A shape's kind is what its fretted notes form: `dim7` (a diminished seventh), `augmented` (an augmented triad), or `any` (three or more distinct notes). The kind never changes as the shape slides. The reference's G♯°7 shape over open E strings is one of the 13 comfortable `dim7` shapes.
  - **Choice**: the kind is drawn by `shape_types` weights, then a shape of that kind. Only shapes whose whole slide fits the section's region are offered, and only those within the comfortable span when any exist, since the stretch is held through the whole slide. If no weighted kind is available, the shape is of kind `any`.
  - **Placement**: the start fret is drawn so every position of the slide stays in the region. If no shape fits a slide that long, a shape that fits the region is used and stops at its edge, and the bar notes why. Every position passes the physical hard rules.

**Etude 1 uses both.** Graph-mode sections open and close the piece, and planing sequences sit between them, as in the reference's bars 1–22, 23–44 and 47–54. With the form rule `S → A B A`, A is graph mode and B is planing mode. The two A sections need not share a center.

Palette entries are chord symbols (`Em`, `F#m7b5/E`, `Bsus4`). Before planning, `chords.py` parses each with `music21.harmony.ChordSymbol` and transposes it to all 12 centers, spelled from the chord: E minor moved to G gives G B♭ D, not G A♯ D.
- **Key spelling**: each center takes the tonic spelling with fewer key-signature accidentals (D♭ major, G♯ minor). Between equal key signatures, it takes the one that gives the palette fewer double sharps and flats (E♭ minor, F♯ major). A chord that would still need a double accidental is respelled on its own.
- **Required tones**: every chord tone except a perfect fifth, plus the bass.
- **Tensions** are off in the first version, so voicings use chord tones only. When enabled, they come from the current center's scale and sound only on `tension_strings`.

**Spelling without a key signature.** The notation has no key signature, so every pitch is written with its accidental, spelled from its chord symbol after transposition. Planing bars have no chord symbol. Their open strings keep plain names, and `score.py` tries every enharmonic spelling of the shape's fretted notes. It keeps the one with the fewest letters shared by two different notes (no B♯ beside an open B), then letters stacked in thirds, then the fewest accidentals. The reference's shape reads G♯ B D F, then A♯ C♯ E G, then D♯ F♯ A C as it slides down.

### Voicer (fretboard.py)

The voicer lists every fingering for a `ChordSpec` in a fret region, removes those that break a hard rule, then samples from the best by score.

Hard rules, all checked:

- Every string the right-hand pattern plays must sound. The pattern uses all six strings, so no string is ever muted.
- Highest fretted note minus lowest fretted note ≤ 5 frets (`max_span`). Open strings don't count.
- No note above fret 13 (`max_fret`). Fret regions end at fret 13.
- At most 4 fretting fingers, counting a barre at the lowest fret as one.
- Every required chord tone sounds; the lowest note is the `ChordSpec`'s bass.
- Tensions only on the strings the config allows, such as strings 1–2.

Soft score, used for ranking:

- Fret movement from the previous bar.
- A span above 3 frets, which the reference never uses (a penalty).
- Open strings (a bonus, as they suit the idiom).
- Doubled tones other than the root and fifth.

The voicer samples among the top candidates with a temperature, so pieces vary but a bad voicing never appears. The weights, the comfortable span, the number of top candidates and the temperature are set in `[voicer]`.

After filtering by pitch class, each string has a handful of options, so a chord has at most a few thousand raw combinations. Results are cached per (`ChordSpec`, region).

**The bass sits on string 6.** All six strings sound and the lowest note must be the bass, so the bass nearly always falls on string 6 at one fixed fret: Gm needs G at fret 3, Cm needs C at fret 8. Each chord is therefore playable only in hand positions that include its bass fret. `inspect --center` shows this for any key. One palette chord has no fingering at all: E♭m/G♭ in E♭ minor. None of its tones is an open string, so all six strings are fretted around a G♭ bass at fret 2, and that takes five fingers.

**Keys that don't fit the region.** Because the center moves, a transposed chord may have no fingering in the current region. The harmony stage offers only chords and centers the voicer can play there, so the walk routes around them. If a `+` or `-` leaves the current center with no playable chord at all, generation stops with an error naming the center and region.

### Rendering and export

- **The right-hand pattern is fixed** to Etude 1's. `render.py` plays it over each bar's fingering and repeats the bar, so the rhythm is always the original's.
- **Held notes**: every note is held to the end of its bar, unless its string is plucked again first, since a string sounds one note at a time. The MIDI carries these durations. The notation writes plain 16ths, with a direction at the start to hold every note to the end of the bar.
- **Accents**: each note of the pattern has its own MIDI velocity, from `[pattern] velocities`. The preset uses the reference's median per note: the bass downbeat loudest, the top of the arpeggio next.
- **Tempo and meter** come from the config. The MIDI slows by `ritardando` over the last bar pair of each section, as the reference does, and the notation marks "rit." and then "a tempo". Nothing about tempo is random.
- `score.py` builds one guitar part in standard notation: single-line 16ths beamed a beat at a time, a treble clef an octave down, and no key signature. The MIDI has no key-signature event either.
- `export.py` writes `out/etude1-<seed>/` containing `piece.mid`, `piece.musicxml` and `manifest.json`.
  - **MIDI** is written with mido, one channel per string, all with the nylon-guitar sound. Two strings holding the same pitch can't cut each other off, as they would on one channel.
  - **The manifest** holds the seed, the full config text, the package's git commit, the derivations, and every bar's center, chord and fingering. `regenerate` rebuilds the piece from it into a `regenerated` folder beside it, and reports whether the MIDI is byte-identical.

Commands:

```
lindenfret generate   --config configs/etude1.toml [--seed N] [--out out/]
lindenfret batch      --config configs/etude1.toml --count 20 [--first-seed N] [--out out/]
lindenfret inspect    --config configs/etude1.toml [--seed N]      # derivation, centers, chord plan, fingerings
lindenfret inspect    --config configs/etude1.toml --chord Em7 [--region 0-4] [--top 10]
lindenfret inspect    --config configs/etude1.toml --center Gm    # where each palette chord is playable
lindenfret regenerate out/etude1-<seed>/manifest.json [--out DIR]
```

## Key design decisions

| Decision | Choice | Why | Alternative considered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source of the musical model | Analysis of the reference MIDI, re-runnable with `tools/analyze_reference.py` | The pattern, form, palette and planing shape come from the real piece, and the facts can be checked | Transcribing by hand from memory |
| Key | No key signature; the tonal center is state the grammar moves | Pieces aren't pinned to E minor, and modulation becomes part of the form | One key per piece; a key-signature change at every modulation |
| What the L-system controls | Form, harmonic motion and modulation, through a defined alphabet | Rules become audible; brackets give departure and return | L-system only sets the bar count |
| Length control | Iterate until the bar count fits the section's range | Keeps the whole self-similar derivation | Fixed iterations, then truncate the string |
| Right-hand pattern | Etude 1's string order as data | Matches the original; a change is a config edit | Generated patterns |
| Voicing search | Enumerate, filter by hard rules, sample by score | Playability guaranteed, and the harmony stage knows what's playable before it chooses | Random sampling with a retry limit |
| Harmony for Etude 1 | Mixed: graph-mode sections open and close the piece, with planing sequences between them | Mirrors the reference's form; graph mode establishes centers and cadences, planing gives the sliding-shape color | Graph only; planing only |
| Fixed material | None: endings, planing shapes and chords are all generated | Every piece is derived entirely from its seed; the reference supplies vocabulary, not content | Copying the reference's closing chords and planing shape |
| Chord definitions | Chord symbols written in E, transposed to each center with music21 before planning | Reads like the reference; music21 spells transposed chords correctly | Roman numerals; hand-typed pitch lists |
| Guitar data | String and fret kept on every note | Enables playability checks and let-ring MIDI | Pitches only |
| music21's role | Chord parsing before planning, and the notation; MIDI is written with mido, one channel per string | Core stays fast and testable without music21; held notes on two strings at the same pitch can't cut each other off; byte-identical MIDI is easy to guarantee | music21 for the MIDI too; mido only, dropping notation |
| Randomness | One `random.Random` per stage, seeded from `hashlib` of (seed, stage name) | Changing the voicer doesn't reshuffle the form. `hashlib`, not `hash()`, because Python salts string hashes per process | One global random stream |
| Config format | TOML via stdlib `tomllib` (Python 3.11+) | No extra dependency; comments allowed | YAML; Python modules |
| Experiments | Named presets in `configs/`, tracked in git | Each variant of the rules is a file or a branch | Copying the script per experiment |

## Testing and validation

- **Unit tests**: fixed rules produce known strings; the same seed gives the same derivation; brackets stay balanced and restore the center; the interpreter maps hand-written strings to the expected plans.
- **Property tests (hypothesis)**: for random `ChordSpec` and region pairs, every fingering the voicer returns passes `check_fingering()`. The checker is written independently of the enumerator, so the voicer never grades itself.
- **Transposition test**: every palette chord transposed to all 12 centers keeps its quality and bass, and is spelled without double sharps or flats.
- **Playability test**: every palette chord in every center has a fingering somewhere on the neck, apart from the known E♭m/G♭ in E♭ minor. A new unplayable chord fails the test.
- **Reference test**: feeding the reference's own fingerings for bars 1–44 through `render.py` reproduces the reference MIDI's pitch in every 16th-note slot.
- **Pattern test**: every rendered bar plays exactly the configured strings, in order, and is repeated as configured.
- **Golden snapshots**: manifests for 3 fixed seeds of `etude1.toml` are checked in under `tests/golden/`, without the config text and git commit. A diff means behavior changed; to accept it on purpose, delete the snapshot and rerun the tests to write a new one.
- **Export round trip**: re-parse the MusicXML with music21 and confirm bar count, pitches, rhythm, beaming and directions survive, and that there is no key signature. The MIDI's tempo map carries the ritardandos.
- **Planing tests**: shapes are classified by what their fretted notes form, each slide keeps one shape shifted by the bar's offset, every position passes the physical rules, and the reference's shape is spelled in stacked thirds.
- **Listening**: the keepers in `curated.toml` are the real acceptance test for musical quality.
